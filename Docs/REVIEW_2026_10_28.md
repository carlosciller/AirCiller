## Findings in progress

- Direct startup previously left demand analysis running; HLS already cancelled
  it. The candidate captures the old task synchronously and awaits its exit
  before any direct preparation work. Waiting must not retire a newer task.
- Fixed cancellation cleanup in `StreamDemandAnalyzer`: readability handlers
  and pipe handles now close on success, cancellation and launch failure.
  A real bundled-ffprobe FIFO regression fails with `LeakedProcessOrPipe` when
  the cleanup is removed, and passes with it. Three cancellations reap the
  process and close both read descriptors. The initial test compilation exposed
  a non-Sendable observer; making the observer explicitly Sendable fixed the
  strict-concurrency failure without relaxing compiler checks.
- A retry while a retired analysis is still exiting also needs its completion
  barrier. `MediaAnalysisTasks` now retains that barrier across cancellation and
  replacement, clearing only its own retirement identity. Focused task tests
  pass for replacement, primary-task independence, Stop, repeated cancellation,
  joining a retired task and preserving a newer movie's demand task.

## First candidate measurement

Private campaign `.build/startup-benchmark/measurement-direct-C1TNfD` completed
with exit 0 and automatic generated-payload cleanup. It uses the same two full
inputs, three alternating pairs each, the pinned engine and bounded hash reader
as the earlier direct-demand experiment. Both sides start actual analysis;
the candidate side cancels and joins it before muxing. Cancellation/join is
included in preparation time. Output bytes are identical within each group.

| Input | Cancel/join preparation, seconds | Concurrent preparation, seconds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Large HDR movie | 9.999, 12.843, 13.006 | 20.205, 21.059, 19.735 |
| HDR episode | 2.083, 2.270, 2.099 | 2.065, 3.956, 2.494 |

Joining took 0.1-0.3 ms in this deliberately aligned-start experiment. This is
not a bound on termination of every process; the cancellation regression also
exercises blocked input. One RSS query failed for the large-movie campaign;
its sampled peak remains a lower bound, not complete memory coverage.

Large-movie preparation medians are 12.843 s cancelled and 20.205 s concurrent;
paired savings are 10.206, 8.217 and 6.729 s. Episode medians are 2.099 s and
2.494 s, with paired savings of -0.018, 1.686 and 0.395 s. The small result
remains variable. Median aggregate CPU is 12.797/13.667 s for the movie and
1.929/2.555 s for the episode (cancelled/concurrent). Sampled aggregate RSS
ranges are 149-177/152-157 MiB and 107-176/123-166 MiB respectively, with the
missing query caveat above. The machine reports macOS 27.2, build 26B123.

The campaign snapshots precede the additional retained-retirement barrier for
immediate retries. Its ordinary single-start scheduling is equivalent, but it
does not establish retry performance or final candidate acceptance. Keep that
distinction in the final source/evidence comparison. No first-picture or
Apple TV savings are inferred from local preparation times.

## Broader review, first pass

- Library admission/season scan and Undo retain explicit membership, do not
  delete originals and preserve current progress when restoring membership.
  Existing malformed-ID handling prevents Undo indexing past its array.
- History loading deliberately discards malformed queue/recent JSON. Current
  tests assert this behavior. Retaining one local recovery copy before reset is
  a potential data-protection improvement for a separate persistence change.
- Playlist audio consent is limited to equivalent codec/profile/layout/rate/
  precision and an available output mode. Stop clears session consent.
  Playback power assertions disable idle system sleep, not display sleep.
- Private command admission bounds command size, deadline and ledger capacity;
  repeated operations cannot execute twice or cross sessions. Full-library
  search is unbounded in result count, a known deferred scalability concern.
- Track drafts reject stale edits and unchanged Apply. Native design review
  must preserve that behavior and the approved system selection model.
